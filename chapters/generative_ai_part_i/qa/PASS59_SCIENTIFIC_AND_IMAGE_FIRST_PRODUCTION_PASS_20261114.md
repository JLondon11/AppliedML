## User-directed visual policy: do not turn every figure into a plot

The scientific-figure portfolio must match the physical meaning of each study. Relevant **genuine real-image** figures include (i) original-source and partition visualizations, (ii) original photographs with task labels or anomalies, (iii) actual named-model input/output/ground truth/error galleries, (iv) spatially annotated image or voxel overlays, and (v) actual-generated-versus-real comparisons that show model failures. Use line/scatter/ECDF/residual maps and numerical diagnostics for quantitative questions; use conceptual/architecture schematics only when they actually teach the equation and mechanism, and label them as conceptual rather than data-driven model results. Do not produce a homogeneous series of simple bar charts. Avoid figures that are decorative, unscientific, untraceable, redundant, or falsely presented as measured outputs.

Every application/case study with image outputs should preferably contain **a model-result gallery plus a relevant quantitative figure**, integrated near their scientific discussion. A source-image montage alone **does not** satisfy executed-model R4 or real-model-results R8: the existing application must separately show the actual model predictions. Conversely, ordinary single-method nonimage tasks may be better explained using a table, curve, or domain diagram. Not every figure needs to be a graph.
